One app on a device

Run this on the device, with the app's local port in place of 8080:

{{template "snippet" "tailscale serve --bg --https=443 8080"}}

On Linux, run it with sudo or allow your user once with sudo tailscale set --operator=$USER.

Tailscale serves the app at https://<device>.<tailnet>.ts.net/. The background Serve mapping survives restarts; keep this computer connected and the app itself running. The tile shows the device's name, title-cased; a label override under that name renames it, and icon changes its icon.

Several apps on one device

Give each app a Tailscale Service. Define svc:myapp with endpoint tcp:443 on the Services page, then run this on a tagged device with Tailscale 1.86 or later:

{{template "snippet" "tailscale serve --service=svc:myapp --https=443 127.0.0.1:8080"}}

Approve the device on the Services page unless your policy approves it automatically. Repeat with a new name for each app.

For Docker apps, DockTail or TSDProxy can publish each container separately. Development-preview ports are not needed for this.

Docker container with DockTail

{{template "snippet" "labels:\n - docktail.service.enable=true\n - docktail.service.name=myapp\n - docktail.service.port=8080"}}

With an OAuth client, DockTail also defines the Service. Without one, define it on the Services page first.

App already on its own device

Apps that already answer HTTPS on port 443 with a valid certificate, such as TSDProxy v2, tsbridge or ScaleTail apps, appear without any of this.

Optional: development previews

Building an app with a coding agent? The tailnet-preview skill can publish temporary development links from the machine hosting your app, including your laptop. Skip this for permanent NAS and container apps.

The publishing machine's owner must approve its Serve ports; your tailnet administrator controls who can connect. With no approved pool, the agent asks once proposing 8443-8452 and waits for approval. To list extra ports, this tailhome's operator configures TAILHOME_PREVIEW_PORTS separately.

Everyone who can open this tailhome sees all listed preview titles, descriptions, addresses and status, even if their app access is blocked. Use metadata this whole audience may read. Hiding a preview does not revoke access.

Unreachable previews, and previews whose app stopped, are hidden. Rings check HTTPS connectivity, not pages. A permanent app appearing here can be kept under Apps with a temporary: false override.
